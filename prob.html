Logic and Probability

Sets as pictures, then the core counting tools behind probability: the multiplication principle, permutations, and combinations, followed by worked examples and practice problems.

Logic: Sets as Pictures

Sets as pictures. The bar over a set means everything in the universe outside it. Put a bar over a bar and you are back where you started.

1. Basic Probability

When every outcome is equally likely, the probability of an event is the number of favorable outcomes divided by the total number of outcomes. 

P(E) = (favorable outcomes) / (total outcomes)

0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1

The complement rule is often the fastest route:

P(not E) = 1 - P(E)

2. The Multiplication Principle

If a task has several steps, and step 1 can be done in n1 ways, step 2 in n2 ways, and so on, then the whole task can be done in: 

n1 × n2 × ... × nk  ways

3. Factorials

n! = n × (n-1) × (n-2) × ... × 2 × 1

0! = 1

n! is the number of ways to arrange n distinct objects in a line.

4. Permutations (order matters)

The number of ways to pick and arrange r objects out of n distinct objects: 

P(n, r) = n! / (n - r)!

Permutations with repeated objects. If some objects are identical, divide out the repeats: 

n! / (a! × b! × c! ...)

Circular arrangements. Arranging n distinct objects around a circle (rotations count as the same): 

(n - 1)!

5. Combinations (order does not matter)

The number of ways to choose r objects out of n, ignoring order: 

C(n, r) = n! / ( r! × (n - r)! )

Note that C(n, r) = C(n, n - r), and P(n, r) = C(n, r) × r!.

Quick Reference

Situation Use
Arrange all n distinct items n!

Choose r of n, order irrelevant C(n, r)
Arrange with identical items n! / (a! b! ...)
Arrange around a circle (n - 1)!
"At least one" 1 - P(none)

Worked Examples

Example 1: Arranging books

In how many ways can 5 different books be placed on a shelf?

5! = 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 120

Example 2: A race

Eight runners compete. How many ways can gold, silver, and bronze be awarded? Order matters, so use a permutation. 

P(8, 3) = 8 × 7 × 6 = 336

Example 3: Repeated letters

How many distinct arrangements of the letters in MISSISSIPPI are there?

There are 11 letters: M × 1, I × 4, S × 4, P × 2. 

11! / (1! × 4! × 4! × 2!) = 39,916,800 / 1,152 = 34,650

Example 4: A committee

How many ways can a committee of 3 be chosen from 10 people? Order does not matter, so use a combination. 

C(10, 3) = (10 × 9 × 8) / (3 × 2 × 1) = 120

Example 5: Coin flips

What is the probability of getting exactly 2 heads in 3 fair coin flips?

Total outcomes     = 2^3 = 8
Favorable outcomes = C(3, 2) = 3

P = 3/8

Example 6: Drawing cards

Two cards are drawn from a standard 52-card deck without replacement. What is the probability that both are aces? 

Favorable = C(4, 2)  = 6
Total     = C(52, 2) = 1,326

P = 6/1326 = 1/221
